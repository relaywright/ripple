# RIPPLE vision

## What this is

RIPPLE is my portfolio piece. It takes what I know from 11+ years in e-commerce and operations (stock runs out, suppliers slip, every fix has a price) and turns it into a polished, working tool that anyone can open in a browser and pick apart.

The product itself is a "what if" simulator for a small, invented supply chain. You break something, and it shows you what happens to stock, sales, and cost under four different responses.

## Who I want it to impress

- **Hiring managers** for ops, e-commerce, and product roles. They should see that I understand real business trade-offs: protecting service costs money, and the right answer depends on the size of the problem.
- **People hiring for AI-assisted building.** They should see that I can direct AI to produce work that is clear, honest, tested, and finished, not a flashy demo that falls apart.
- **Technical reviewers.** They should be able to run it in two commands and find that the code, tests, and claims hold up.

## What success looks like

1. **Clear in 30 seconds.** A stranger landing on the live site or the GitHub page understands what RIPPLE does, what question it answers, and who it's for, without anyone explaining it.
2. **The trade-offs make sense to an operator.** Someone with real ops experience nods along: the costs, delays, and responses behave the way the real world roughly does.
3. **The craft is visible.** The docs read plainly, every claim matches the code, the tests pass, and it works on a phone and with a keyboard.
4. **It's easy to show off.** The walkthrough video, screenshots, and link preview all reflect the current app and make someone want to click.

## What it is NOT

- **Not an enterprise tool.** No multi-product planning, live data feeds, or integrations. It stays small enough to read and understand.
- **No accounts, sign-ups, or tracking.** Free, private, and it runs entirely in the browser.
- **No AI inside the app, for now.** That would need a server or an API key, which breaks the "just open it" promise.
- **No made-up credibility.** No claims of real customers, real savings, or that I hand-wrote the code. AI built it under my direction, and the repo says so.

## Open to later

- Letting people plug in their own numbers or network, as long as the app stays honest that it's a teaching model, not a forecast.

## Current priorities, in order

1. **Clarity.** The first 30 seconds on the site and the README come first.
2. **Presentation and sharing.** Keep the video, screenshots, and link preview current, and get it in front of people.
3. **Credibility.** Claims match the code; tests and checks stay green.
4. **New depth** only when it serves the three above.
